Florida well water testing: county health departments and certified labs

Short answer: Call your county health department: the Florida Department of Health says it can explain how to collect samples and have them tested, usually for $20 to $30 per sample. You can also search Florida DEP's list of NELAP-certified labs. Test for bacteria and nitrate at least once a year.

The U.S. Geological Survey estimated that about 2.46 million people in Florida supplied their own household water in 2015. USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source] [source]

What to test and how often

The Florida Department of Health strongly recommends testing a private well for bacteria and nitrate at least once per year, calling it a simple and inexpensive step. [source]

Where to test

  • Your county health department can tell you how to collect the samples yourself and have them tested, usually for $20–30 per sample. [source]
  • Certified labs: search Florida DEP's NELAP certified laboratory search. [source]

Free sampling near contamination sites

Florida DEP's Water Supply Restoration Funding program pays county health units to survey and sample private wells at no cost to the property owner. It's not on request: priority goes to areas where contamination appears to threaten health, such as farm areas or sites near underground storage tanks. [source]

How much does well water testing cost in Florida?

Does Florida test private wells for free?

Florida DEP funds no-cost sampling by county health units, but only in areas of known or suspected contamination, prioritized by public health risk.
