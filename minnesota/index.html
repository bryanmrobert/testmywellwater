Minnesota well water testing: MDH recommendations

Short answer: Use a lab from the Minnesota Department of Health's accredited laboratory search, or ask your county environmental or public health services whether they offer well testing. MDH recommends testing every year for coliform bacteria and nitrate, and at least once for arsenic, lead and manganese, at the faucet you drink from.

The U.S. Geological Survey estimated that about 1.18 million people in Minnesota supplied their own household water in 2015. USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source] [source]

Test water from a faucet you use for cooking and drinking, and if you treat your water, test it after treatment. Testing is especially important if babies or young children drink the water. [source]

Arsenic and new wells

Since August 2008, well contractors have tested each newly drilled well for arsenic and shared the results with the owner and MDH. MDH says to use an accredited laboratory, or ask your county environmental or public health services whether they offer well testing. [source]

FAQ

How do I get my well water tested in Minnesota?

Use a lab from the Minnesota Department of Health's accredited laboratory search, or ask your county environmental or public health services whether they offer well testing. MDH recommends testing every year for coliform bacteria and nitrate, and at least once for arsenic, lead and manganese, at the faucet you drink from.

Are new Minnesota wells tested for arsenic?

Yes. MDH says that since August 2008, well contractors test each newly drilled well for arsenic and share the results with the owner and MDH.
