Anoka County, MN well water testing: county program prices

Short answer: Pick up a kit at the Anoka County Government Center, 2100 3rd Ave N, Suite 600, Anoka, or a participating city hall. Drop off the sample and pay in person Mondays 8:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m. or Tuesdays 8:30–11:30 a.m. The county charges $17 each for coliform bacteria and nitrate.

Anoka County estimates about 25,000 private wells in service throughout the county, and says only a small percentage of them are tested each year. [source] Its well-testing brochure says over 30% of county residents are served by a private water well. [source] USGS's 2015 county estimate put the self-supplied population at about 83,000. [source]

Testing a private well is the owner's responsibility. Anoka County Public Health and Environmental Services runs a well water testing program through a contract with a state-certified water testing laboratory. [source] [source]

What to test

Anoka County Environmental Services and MDH recommend testing all well water for coliform bacteria and nitrate every year, lead at least once, and manganese before a baby drinks the water. [source] MDH also recommends testing at least once for arsenic. [source] All of these are on the county's price list. [source]

Nitrate: the county says nitrate-nitrogen occurs naturally in groundwater below 1 mg/L and can seep into wells from septic systems, fertilizers, animal feedlots and landfills. MDH's Health Risk Limit is 10 mg/L; above that, levels may pose an immediate risk to infants and pregnant women. [source] See our nitrate guide.

Arsenic: the county says most Anoka County residents have some measurable arsenic in their well water. It has no taste or odor, so testing is the only way to know. EPA's limit for public water is 10 µg/L, and EPA's goal is 0. Typical water softeners and activated carbon filters won't remove arsenic on their own, and boiling only concentrates it; reverse osmosis, specialty media and distillation are the most common treatments. [source]

If coliform or E. coli is found

If total coliform is present and E. coli is absent, the county says to disinfect the well and building plumbing, including any treatment equipment; instructions come with results from the county program. A licensed well contractor can do it, or the owner can. Resample at least 10 days after disinfecting, and consult a licensed well contractor if two attempts still leave coliform present. [source]

If E. coli is present, the county recommends retesting right away, preferably at a private state-accredited lab for faster results, disinfecting before that result comes back, and using boiled or bottled water for cooking and drinking until the water no longer tests positive. Boiling can concentrate nitrate or arsenic if those are also high. [source] See our coliform guide.

FAQ

Where can I get my well water tested in Anoka County, Minnesota?

Pick up a kit at the Anoka County Government Center, 2100 3rd Ave N, Suite 600, Anoka, or a participating city hall. Drop off the sample and pay in person Mondays 8:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m. or Tuesdays 8:30–11:30 a.m. The county charges $17 each for coliform bacteria and nitrate.

How much does well water testing cost in Anoka County, MN?

Anoka County's Well Water Testing page lists $17 each for coliform bacteria and nitrates, so the yearly sanitary analysis is $34. Arsenic, manganese, copper, iron and fluoride are $17 each, lead $18, total hardness $35, mercury $45 and iron bacteria $60.

Where do I drop off a well water sample in Anoka County?

At the Environmental Services front desk on the 6th floor of the Anoka County Government Center, 2100 3rd Ave N, Anoka, in person on Mondays 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. or Tuesdays 8:30 to 11:30 a.m., excluding holidays. Payment is due at drop-off.

How long do Anoka County well water test results take?

The county currently estimates about 3 to 4 weeks. If you need results sooner, for example for a real estate sale, it suggests a private state-accredited lab.
