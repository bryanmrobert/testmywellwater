Georgia well water testing: DPH recommendations and UGA Extension tests

Short answer: Go to your UGA County Cooperative Extension office for kits. Georgia's Department of Public Health recommends the W-35 coliform test every year, also offered by county public health departments, and the W-33C chemical screen, including arsenic and lead, every three years. UGA Extension's Oconee office lists W-33C at $122 and W-35 at $42.

The U.S. Geological Survey estimated that about 1.51 million people in Georgia supplied their own household water in 2015. USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source] [source]

Georgia's Department of Public Health (DPH) says private wells in Georgia are not subject to any regulatory authority, so the owner is responsible for monitoring water quality. [source]

FAQ

How do I get my well water tested in Georgia?

Go to your UGA County Cooperative Extension office for kits. Georgia's Department of Public Health recommends the W-35 coliform test every year, also offered by county public health departments, and the W-33C chemical screen, including arsenic and lead, every three years. UGA Extension's Oconee office lists W-33C at $122 and W-35 at $42.

Are private wells regulated in Georgia?

No. Georgia DPH says private water wells are not subject to any regulatory authority, so it is the owner's responsibility to monitor water quality.
