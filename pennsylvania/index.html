Pennsylvania well water testing: Penn State lab packages and fees

How do I test my well water in Pennsylvania? Step 1: Get a Penn State drinking water test kit at a county extension office, or a bacteria kit from your DEP regional office. Step 2: Collect the sample following the instructions and overnight it to Penn State's DEP-accredited lab; bacteria samples must arrive within 30 hours. Packages cost $60 to $220, and results take about 10 to 14 business days. You can also pick any lab from DEP's list of accredited laboratories.

The U.S. Geological Survey estimated that about 3.47 million people in Pennsylvania supplied their own household water in 2015. USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source] [source]

Penn State's Agricultural Analytical Services Laboratory (AASL) is accredited by the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection for drinking water analysis. [source]

How to get a kit

You first need a Penn State Drinking Water Test Kit, available at many county extension offices. It includes a cooler, shipping box, sample bottles and instructions. [source] See our sampling guide.

Timing

Samples must reach the lab within 30 hours of sampling and no later than Thursday of any given week. [source] The lab lists closures November 26–27, 2026 and December 21, 2026 – January 2, 2027.

Gas drilling documentation: for before/after gas-drilling documentation, Penn State recommends an accredited lab that can collect the sample with full chain of custody. [source]

Contact: aaslab@psu.edu, 814-863-0841, 720 Tower Rd, University Park, PA 16802. [source]

How fast must the sample reach the lab?

Within 30 hours of sampling, and no later than Thursday of any given week.

Where can I find an official list of certified labs for well water testing in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania DEP says to use a state-accredited lab for drinking water. Its Laboratory Accreditation Program search is at https://www.pa.gov/services/dep/apply-for-laboratory-accreditation, and its private well page with lab lists is at https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/residents/my-water/private-wells/water-testing.

Which health department pages should I use for private well testing in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania DEP, not a state health department, oversees private well guidance: see https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dep/residents/my-water/private-wells/water-testing. Chester County's Health Department lists local testing labs at https://www.chesco.org/978/Well-Water-Test-Labs.
