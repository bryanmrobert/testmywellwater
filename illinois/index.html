Illinois well water testing: health departments and IDPH help

Short answer: Contact your local health department first, since the Illinois Department of Public Health says most can help test for bacteria and nitrate. For other contaminants, use a commercial lab. IDPH helps you choose a lab and interprets results for free at 217-782-5830, and the Illinois State Water Survey lab is at 217-333-9234.

The U.S. Geological Survey estimated that about 1.15 million people in Illinois supplied their own household water in 2015. USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source] [source]

When to test

IDPH says to test your well when there are known well water problems in the area, after flooding, land disturbance or nearby waste disposal, after repairing or replacing any part of the well system, and when the water's taste, color or smell changes. [source]

Where to test

  • Local health department: most can help test for bacteria and nitrate. [source] [source]
  • Commercial labs: may be the only option for some contaminants, and IDPH can help you select one. [source]
  • Help with results: IDPH interprets results and gives recommendations for free: 217-782-5830. [source] Illinois EPA also lists the Illinois State Water Survey's Public Service Laboratory, 217-333-9234. [source]

If something is found

IDPH's advice: if bacteria are found, boil water before using it for drinking and cooking. If nitrate is found, do not boil the water, because boiling concentrates nitrate. IDPH notes that nitrate is linked to "blue baby syndrome" in infants six months old or younger. [source]
