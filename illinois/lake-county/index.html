Lake County, IL well water testing: county lab prices

Short answer: Pick up a sterile bottle at the Central Permit Facility, 500 W. Winchester Road, Libertyville, or one of the county's water sample kit sites. Return it to the Libertyville lab Monday–Thursday, 8:00–3:30. The county fee schedule lists the bacteria test (with a nitrate screen) at $25, or $71 for a staff home visit.

About 90,000 Lake County residents rely on groundwater from aquifers to supply their private wells, and another 146,000 rely on community water systems supplied by wells, according to the Lake County Health Department. [source] USGS's 2015 county estimate put the self-supplied population at about 87,000. [source]

The Health Department's Environmental Laboratory tests well water for Lake County residents, and its well program inspects and monitors private wells. [source] [source] The county says every year there are instances of contaminated drinking water in Lake County, and private wells should be tested at least annually. [source]

How to collect the sample

  1. Use only a Lake County Health Department sample bottle.
  2. Pick a cold-water fixture without leaks; remove aerators or tap filters, and avoid hoses and outside faucets if you can. If you drink softened water, sample the softened water.
  3. Run the cold water wide open for at least five minutes, until it turns noticeably colder. Don't flame the faucet.
  4. Reduce the flow to a steady, pencil-width stream, open the bottle without touching the rim or inside, and fill it to the top of the shoulder.
  5. Fill in the lab form, including the sample location, the site address, a mailing address and phone numbers. Results can be emailed, faxed or mailed.

Steps summarized from the county's sampling instructions. [source]

If coliform or E. coli is found

Lake County says not to use water with coliform bacteria for drinking, brushing teeth or washing foods that won't be cooked; if E. coli is present, body contact with the water is not advised. The Health Department will inspect the well for damage or flooding in these cases. The well and plumbing must be chlorinated, preferably by a licensed water well contractor, the chlorine flushed out, and a new sample collected. [source] The county posts a list of licensed water well contractors. [source] IDPH adds that if bacteria are found, boil water before using it for drinking and cooking. [source] See our coliform guide.

Nitrate, fluoride, lead and arsenic

Nitrate: the county says high nitrate is uncommon in Lake County, but levels above 10 milligrams per liter can be harmful to infants. If an infant or a pregnant mother lives in the household, test for nitrate at least once a year. [source] IDPH says not to boil nitrate-contaminated water, because boiling concentrates nitrate. [source] See our nitrate guide.

Fluoride: fluoride occurs naturally in groundwater, and in some areas natural levels may be higher than in community water. The county says to have a well's fluoride level tested before giving a child a fluoride supplement. [source]

Lead: household plumbing is the most common source of lead in drinking water, and the county cites the 15 parts per billion federal limit. Boiling will not remove lead. To reduce exposure, use cold water for drinking and cooking, never mix baby formula with hot tap water, and run cold water for three minutes or more first. [source] [source]

Arsenic: arsenic can enter groundwater from natural deposits or from farm and industrial practices; EPA's drinking-water standard is 10 parts per billion. Well owners can pick up sterile metals bottles and return them to the Environmental Laboratory in Libertyville. [source]

Abandoned wells

Wells no longer used must be sealed by an Illinois licensed water well contractor or the property owner, under a Health Department permit approved before the work. Notify the department at least 24 hours before sealing. Keeping a well for outdoor use when you have another primary water source requires Health Department approval. [source]

FAQ

Where can I get my well water tested in Lake County, Illinois?

Pick up a sterile bottle at the Central Permit Facility, 500 W. Winchester Road, Libertyville, or one of the county's water sample kit sites. Return it to the Libertyville lab Monday–Thursday, 8:00–3:30. The county fee schedule lists the bacteria test (with a nitrate screen) at $25, or $71 for a staff home visit.

How much does well water testing cost in Lake County, IL?

The Lake County Health Department's 2026 fee schedule lists the bacteriological analysis at $25; the standard kit test covers total coliform, E. coli and a nitrate screen. A staff visit to collect the sample costs $71. Other tests include fluoride $15, analytical nitrate $18, and arsenic, lead or copper $55 each.

Where do I drop off a well water sample in Lake County, IL?

At the Lake County Central Permit Facility, 500 W. Winchester Road, Libertyville, Monday through Thursday, 8:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Samples are not accepted Friday through Sunday and must be analyzed within 24 hours of collection.

How long do Lake County well water test results take?

The county's sampling instructions say lab testing takes 24 to 72 hours, and its March 2026 release says results are ready in about three business days. Metals analyses take about two to three weeks.
