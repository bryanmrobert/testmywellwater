North Carolina well water testing: NCDHHS schedule and certified labs

Short answer: Call your local health department, which schedules testing through the North Carolina State Laboratory of Public Health; procedures and fees vary by county. Or choose a lab from the State Laboratory's certified laboratory search. NCDHHS recommends coliform testing every year and metals, nitrates, lead and copper every two years. NCDHHS questions: 919-707-5900.

The U.S. Geological Survey estimated that about 2.41 million people in North Carolina supplied their own household water in 2015. USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source] [source]

Testing schedule (NCDHHS)

How oftenTest for
Every yearTotal and fecal coliform bacteria; also inspect the wellhead
Every two yearsHeavy metals, nitrates, nitrites, lead and copper
Every five yearsPesticides and volatile organic compounds (yearly if a known pesticide is applied nearby)

Also test after repairs, floods, a change in taste, color or odor, or if someone is pregnant or there's an infant at home (nitrates). [source]

Contaminants North Carolina highlights

NCDHHS says many wells in North Carolina have too much lead, arsenic, iron and manganese, low pH, and harmful bacteria. [source]

Where to test

  • Your local health department can schedule a test through the North Carolina State Laboratory of Public Health. Procedures and fees vary by county. [source]
  • New wells must be tested before use, with bacterial and chemical tests within 30 days of completion. [source]
  • Certified labs: State Laboratory certified laboratory search. [source]
  • Questions: NCDHHS Private Well and Health Team, 919-707-5900. Its free StoryMap shows well test results by county. [source]

When a well exceeds health-based standards, the local health department and DHHS explain the results and next steps to the owner. [source]

FAQ

How do I get my well water tested in North Carolina?

Call your local health department, which schedules testing through the North Carolina State Laboratory of Public Health; procedures and fees vary by county. Or choose a lab from the State Laboratory's certified laboratory search. NCDHHS recommends coliform testing every year and metals, nitrates, lead and copper every two years. NCDHHS questions: 919-707-5900.

Do new wells in North Carolina have to be tested?

Yes. NCDHHS says all new private wells must be tested by the State Laboratory of Public Health or a state-certified commercial lab before use, within 30 days of completion.

What contaminants are common in North Carolina wells?

NCDHHS says many NC wells have too much lead, arsenic, iron, manganese, low pH and harmful bacteria.
