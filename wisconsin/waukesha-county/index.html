Waukesha County, WI well water testing: county lab prices

Short answer: Use the Waukesha County Environmental Health Division's state-certified lab. Order a kit online or by phone at (262) 896-8300, or pick one up at its Waukesha office. A coliform bacteria test costs $35 ($40 if the kit is mailed to you), nitrate $30 and fluoride $20. The lab accepts samples Monday through Thursday, 8 a.m. to 3 p.m.

Waukesha County says it has over 40,000 private wells, but most are not tested for coliform bacteria and other contaminants on the recommended yearly basis. [source] USGS's 2015 county estimate put the self-supplied population at about 132,000. [source]

What and how often to test

  • Every year: the county encourages testing for coliform bacteria every year, and suggests making it a springtime routine. [source] [source]
  • Nitrate: test if someone in the home is pregnant or there's an infant under 6 months. [source]
  • Also test after the well is modified, after flooding, or when the water's taste, odor or appearance changes. [source] [source]

Statewide, Wisconsin DHS recommends testing for bacteria and nitrate at least once a year, and for arsenic and lead at least once every five years. [source] Since the county lab doesn't list arsenic or lead for homeowner kits, use another state-certified lab for those. [source] [source]

Reading your results

  • Bacteria: an "unsafe" result means coliform bacteria were found in the sample. [source]
  • Nitrate: above 10 mg/l is a health risk to infants 6 months and younger. Don't give the water to infants or use it to make formula, and don't boil it, because boiling concentrates nitrate. [source] For high nitrate, the county says to contact a well driller or plumber. [source] See our nitrate guide.
  • Fluoride: share the result with your doctor or dentist. [source]
  • Hardness: the county calls water above 100 mg/l "hard" and below 50 mg/l "soft." [source]

Groundwater in Waukesha County

The county's 2022 Land and Water Resource Management Plan says most of Waukesha County has moderate to high potential for groundwater contamination in the shallow glacial drift and Niagara aquifers. The most vulnerable areas are where both the Niagara dolomite and the water table are near the surface. Isolated cases of contamination have been traced to runoff, septic system discharges and chemical spills or leakage. [source]

If coliform is found

The county says to resample to confirm. If the second sample is also unsafe, don't drink the water unless it's been boiled at a rolling boil for at least 5 minutes. If there's no obvious source of contamination, have a well driller, pump installer or the owner disinfect the system. If there are obvious sources, or disinfection doesn't work, call (262) 896-8300 to speak with a sanitarian. [source]

The county's chlorination procedure uses 1 gallon of 5% laundry bleach (or 10 ounces of dry calcium hypochlorite) per 100 gallons of water in the well. Bypass the water softener, run each faucet until you smell chlorine, and let it stand 24 hours. Then run the water to a spot away from the house, usually the roadside ditch, until the chlorine odor is gone. Wait a week to 10 days before resampling, and if it's clean, take a second sample 1–2 months later. Don't chlorinate the well yourself if the pump or piping connects to the well cap or the well is flowing or pressurized; the county says to use a licensed pump installer or well driller because of the risk of pump damage and electric shock. [source] See our coliform guide.

Buying or refinancing a home with a well

The county offers well and septic evaluations for property sales and refinancing: a visual check of the system plus a bacteria test. Email the application to sod@waukeshacounty.gov. [source] Submit it at least 2 weeks before your deadline. The county's sanitarians are certified by the DNR as Private Well Inspectors, and the evaluation checks the well against Wisconsin's NR 812 well code. [source]

Unused wells

Since June 1, 2008, only licensed well drillers and pump installers may abandon (fill and seal) a well. Cost-sharing grants may be available through the county's Land Resources Division; call Environmental Health at 262-896-8300 for details. [source]

Help paying for testing

Through Wisconsin's Well Testing Access Program (WellTAP), well users may be eligible for no-cost testing through their local or Tribal health department. The well must serve your primary residence and someone who is pregnant, a child 12 or under, or a person with a weakened immune system, and a financial or other burden must keep you from testing on your own. Contact your local health department to check eligibility. [source]

Other certified labs

The DNR's list of labs certified to test drinking water for bacteria includes the Waukesha County lab and Eurofins Microbiology Laboratories in New Berlin, (262) 727-6006. [source] Call any lab first to confirm it accepts private well samples. For other contaminants, see the DNR's certified laboratory lists, and get bacteria samples to the lab within the 48 hours the DNR says an accurate test requires. [source] [source]
