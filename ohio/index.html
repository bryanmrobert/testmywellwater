Ohio well water testing: what to test every year

Short answer: Ohio leaves testing to owners, so choose a testing lab, use its own bottle and follow its directions, checking its schedule because some labs won't accept samples on Fridays or weekends. Ohio State University Extension recommends yearly tests for total coliform bacteria, nitrate, pH and total dissolved solids. Report suspected contamination to your local health department.

The U.S. Geological Survey estimated that about 1.86 million people in Ohio supplied their own household water in 2015. USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source] [source]

Annual tests (OSU Extension)

Water tests aren't required in Ohio, but OSU Extension says a private well should be tested at least once a year for:

  • total coliform bacteria
  • nitrate
  • pH
  • total dissolved solids (or conductivity) [source]

It says these four tests are reasonable in cost and show either good water or a problem that needs more specific testing. Keep yearly records to spot trends. [source]

Where to test

Owners are on their own to find a testing lab, collect a sample and act on the results. Use the lab's own bottle, follow its directions, and check its schedule, since some labs won't accept samples on Fridays or weekends. FAQ

How do I get my well water tested in Ohio?

Ohio leaves testing to owners, so choose a testing lab, use its own bottle and follow its directions, checking its schedule because some labs won't accept samples on Fridays or weekends. Ohio State University Extension recommends yearly tests for total coliform bacteria, nitrate, pH and total dissolved solids. Report suspected contamination to your local health department.

Is well water testing required in Ohio?

OSU Extension says water tests are not required for an existing private well, but it should be tested at least once a year.
