Geauga County, OH well water testing: health district sampling and the county water lab

Short answer: Geauga Public Health staff will collect a coliform sample at your home for $90; request it with the Household Water Systems Evaluation form or call Environmental Health at 440-279-1914. Or buy a $27.50 total coliform bottle from the county Department of Water Resources, 12611 Ravenwood Drive, Suite 390, Chardon, and drop it at its Aquilla Road lab.

Most Geauga County residents get their water from private wells. A USGS report prepared with the Geauga County Planning Commission says 98 percent of residents rely on ground water for drinking water and most of the population (78 percent) is served by self-supplied domestic wells. [source] USGS's 2015 county estimate put the self-supplied population at about 72,000, roughly 76 percent of residents; USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source]

That same USGS report notes that regular water-quality testing of private wells isn't mandated by local, state or federal rules, which cover only public water suppliers serving 25 or more people. [source] Testing is up to the owner, and Geauga County has two county options.

Option 1: Geauga Public Health collects the sample

ItemDetails

What you getA Household Water Systems Evaluation: staff collect a water sample for coliform bacteria, and an inspector gives a professional opinion on whether the water supply system is satisfactory, based on records, the owner, the field visit and lab results. The opinion doesn't assess the amount of water or its chemical quality. [source]

Before the visitSomeone needs to provide access. A $55 revisit fee applies if the building was unattended and a sample couldn't be collected, or if the sample contained excessive chlorine. [source]
ResultsMailed to the address on the form. If a sample isn't acceptable, the department requires one consecutive acceptable sample. [source]
Standard usedThe form cites the Ohio Department of Health standard of four or fewer total coliform units and zero detectable E. coli. [source]
ContactGeauga Public Health, 12611 Ravenwood Dr., Floor 3, Room A301, Chardon, OH 44024 (mail: Suite 300). Environmental Health 440-279-1914; main office 440-279-1900. [source]

Option 2: Geauga County Department of Water Resources lab (you collect)

ItemDetails
Who runs itGeauga County Department of Water Resources (GCDWR). Its lab handles water and wastewater analysis, and the department says each analyst holds State of Ohio water and wastewater certifications. Its most requested drinking water test is total coliform bacteria, the "water potability test." [source]
Getting a bottleBuy a pre-sterilized bottle at the Department Office, 12611 Ravenwood Drive, Suite 390, Chardon, Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–4 p.m. A bottle must be bought before water is drawn. [source] Only GCDWR bottles are accepted, payment is due when you pick up the bottle, and bottles aren't refundable. Bring exact change if paying cash. [source] [source]
CollectingUse a faucet without a leak, swivel joint or sprayer, and remove the aerator; hoses and drinking fountains aren't acceptable. Put carbon filters, sediment filters and softeners on bypass. Sanitize the tap with an unscented household bleach solution, flush 3–5 minutes, fill the bottle to the top without overflowing, and complete the bacterial sample report form. [source]
Dropping offOnly at the GCDWR Laboratory, 13335 Aquilla Rd., Chardon (a long driveway just south of Ravenwood Drive). Total coliform: Monday–Wednesday 7 a.m.–4 p.m. and Thursday 7 a.m.–noon, through the small sample drop-off door left of the lab entrance. Other tests: Monday–Thursday 7 a.m.–4 p.m. No tests are accepted on Fridays. [source] Return a bacteria sample within 12 hours of collection. [source]
ResultsIf total coliform is positive, the lab notifies you by phone or email about 24–48 hours after drop-off; negative results are mailed or emailed. Some samples need confirmation, which can take another 96 hours. A phone call with negative results costs an extra $2. [source]
ContactDepartment office 440-279-1970; lab 440-279-1975. [source]

What and how often to test

The Geauga Soil and Water Conservation District tells well owners to test annually, or when the water's appearance, taste or smell changes, for total coliform bacteria, E. coli, nitrates and arsenic, plus contaminants of local concern, and to keep records of every test. [source] Ohio State University Extension says to test at least once a year for total coliform bacteria, nitrate, pH and total dissolved solids or conductivity. [source] GCDWR's posted fee list doesn't include arsenic, so use another certified lab for that test. See our nitrate guide and coliform guide.

Local water quality issues

USGS's review of Geauga County groundwater studies found that iron and manganese often exceed U.S. EPA's secondary (aesthetic) limits. Road salt and, less commonly, oil-field brines and volatile organic compounds had been found at isolated locations. Nitrate had not been detected above the 10 mg/L federal limit, though some levels suggested fertilizer or septic effects. [source]

In USGS's 1999 sampling of 31 wells, 16 exceeded the county health district's standard of zero total coliform colonies; iron exceeded the secondary limit in 19 wells and manganese in 18, and hydrogen sulfide (the rotten-egg smell) was detected in 17. Those results are from 1999, so test your own well rather than assume. [source]

If coliform is found

GCDWR says a well chlorination should be done at your earliest opportunity if total coliform is present in private well water. [source] The Ohio Department of Health says disinfection should be done after a well is found coliform-positive on a presence/absence test, after total coliform is above four colony-forming units, or if E. coli is present, and that a homeowner may carry out the procedure on an existing well. [source]

New wells and records

A homeowner or builder starting a new home must contact Geauga Public Health for a lot evaluation, after which sewage and water well permits are issued. Private water system permits are good for one year; in that time the installation or alteration must pass a final inspection and a water test for coliform bacteria and nitrates. [source] Geauga Public Health also has an online search of septic and private water system records by address. [source]

Other certified labs

The Geauga SWCD brochure lists the Geauga County Water Resources Lab and Biosolutions, (440) 708-2999, as testing labs. [source] You can also use a laboratory on Ohio EPA's combined list of certified drinking water labs. [source]

FAQ

Where can I get my well water tested in Geauga County, Ohio?

Geauga Public Health staff will collect a coliform sample at your home for $90; request it with the Household Water Systems Evaluation form or call Environmental Health at 440-279-1914. Or buy a $27.50 total coliform bottle from the county Department of Water Resources, 12611 Ravenwood Drive, Suite 390, Chardon, and drop it at its Aquilla Road lab.

How much does well water testing cost in Geauga County, OH?

Geauga Public Health charges $90 for a staff-collected coliform sample and household water system evaluation ($55 visit plus $35 lab), and $90 per re-sample. The county Department of Water Resources lab charges $27.50 for a total coliform test you collect yourself, $22.50 for nitrate and $27.50 for lead under its 2026 fee schedule.

Where do I drop off a well water sample in Geauga County?

Buy the bottle at the Department of Water Resources office, 12611 Ravenwood Drive, Suite 390, Chardon, then drop the sample only at the department's lab, 13335 Aquilla Road, Chardon. Total coliform samples are taken Monday to Wednesday 7 a.m. to 4 p.m. and Thursday 7 a.m. to noon; nothing is accepted on Fridays.

How fast do I need to get a Geauga County bacteria sample to the lab?

The Department of Water Resources says to return a total coliform sample within 12 hours of drawing the water. Positive results are phoned or emailed about 24 to 48 hours after drop-off.

How many Geauga County residents use private wells?
