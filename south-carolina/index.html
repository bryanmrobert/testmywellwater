South Carolina well water testing: SCDES kits and fees

Short answer: Pick up a kit from your local SCDES regional office or health department. SCDES charges $20 for a coliform bacteria test and $50 for metals and minerals, and people over 65 or on public assistance get one of each free yearly. For real-estate testing, SCDES recommends a certified commercial lab from its CELMapp.

The U.S. Geological Survey estimated that about 1.18 million people in South Carolina supplied their own household water in 2015. USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source] [source]

SCDES does not regulate the water quality of private wells. [source]

SCDES testing services

TestFeeResults
Coliform bacteria (with E. coli if coliform is found)$20By mail in two to three weeks
Metals and minerals: alkalinity, calcium, chloride, copper, hardness, iron, lead, magnesium, manganese, nitrate/nitrite, pH, zinc$50By mail in six to eight weeks

Get a kit from your local SCDES regional office or health department. SCDES accepts samples Mondays, Tuesdays and Wednesdays, and you're invoiced with the results. SCDES says it isn't accepting metals and minerals samples from June 1 to October 31, 2026, with a tentative restart on November 1. [source]

Free and reduced-cost options

  • People over 65 or on public assistance (such as food stamps, WIC or Medicaid) can get one coliform test and one metals and minerals analysis free each year.
  • Fees may be waived or reduced based on ability to pay.
  • Private well owners can apply for an SCDES PFAS assessment. If PFAS is found, owners may be eligible for filtration or funding to connect to public water. [source]

Real-estate testing

SCDES recommends not using these tests for real-estate transactions. Find a certified commercial lab with SCDES CELMapp. [source] [source]
