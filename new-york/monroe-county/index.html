Monroe County, NY well water testing: county health department sampling

Short answer: Call the Monroe County Department of Public Health at 585-753-5057 to schedule. County staff collect the sample and take it to the Monroe County Environmental Lab; the department won't accept samples you collect yourself. Its 2026 request form lists a $35 trip fee plus $60 for a bacteria test or $230 for the private well base package.

How the county program works

ItemDetails
Who runs itMonroe County Department of Public Health. Staff collect water samples from private drinking wells, and the samples are tested at the Monroe County Environmental Lab. [source]
How to orderCall 585-753-5057 to set up an appointment, or email water@monroecounty.gov. The county's Water Sampling Request Form lists the sampling options and prices. [source] [source]
Self-collected samplesNot accepted. The department says sample collection and transportation affect sample integrity, so its own staff must collect samples and take them to the lab. [source]

The form says a nitrate (NO3) result requires two tests, combined nitrate/nitrite and nitrite, so selecting nitrate costs $100. Suite 1 includes one lead sample, collected as first draw unless you ask for a flush sample instead. [source]

What and how often to test

The department recommends testing for coliform bacteria once a year, and every 3–5 years for lead, nitrate, nitrite, arsenic, sodium, iron, manganese, turbidity, pH, hardness and alkalinity. It also suggests testing when:

  • the water looks, smells or tastes different
  • someone is pregnant or a baby is expected
  • a health care provider suspects the water may be causing a health condition
  • the well was flooded or damaged by extreme weather
  • the well runs dry or water flow changes [source]

NYSDOH says late spring or early summer is the best time to test for coliform, since contamination is most likely to show up in wet weather. [source]

Reading your results

Private wells aren't regulated like public water, but the county says the State Sanitary Code, Subpart 5-1, and NYS Appendix 75-C can be used as guidance. Its quick reference guide lists these levels: [source]

If coliform is found

The county says disinfection may be needed if a sample tests positive for coliform: a chlorine solution must circulate through, and stay in, the well and house plumbing for a while. [source] NYSDOH advises boiling water until disinfection and retesting confirm the contamination is gone. [source] NYSDOH's step-by-step method uses unscented household bleach and recommends hiring a water well contractor for point wells or jet pumps. [source] See our coliform guide.

Other options

You can use another lab certified by NYSDOH's Environmental Laboratory Approval Program; search the NYSDOH ELAP lab search. [source] Cornell Cooperative Extension Monroe County lists a water testing drop-off site in Rochester at Lozier Environmental Consulting, 2011 East Main Street, 585-654-9080, open weekdays 8 a.m.–4 p.m. The network is set up mainly for farm water testing, but CCE says well water tests are also offered, with bottles and forms at the site. [source]

FAQ

Where can I get my well water tested in Monroe County, New York?

Call the Monroe County Department of Public Health at 585-753-5057 to schedule. County staff collect the sample and take it to the Monroe County Environmental Lab; the department won't accept samples you collect yourself. How much does a well water test cost in Monroe County, NY?

The Monroe County Department of Public Health's 2026 Water Sampling Request Form lists a $35 per-trip sampling fee on every request, plus $60 for a bacteria test, $230 for the Private Well Base Package and $165 for the Expanded Package.

Can I drop off my own well water sample with Monroe County?

No. The department says it cannot accept privately collected samples; its staff must collect the sample and take it to the lab. Call 585-753-5057 to schedule.

How often should Monroe County well owners test?

The Monroe County Department of Public Health recommends testing for coliform bacteria once a year and for lead, nitrate, arsenic, sodium, iron, manganese and other parameters every 3 to 5 years.
