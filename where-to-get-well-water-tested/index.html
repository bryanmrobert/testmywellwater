Where to get well water tested

Short answer: You have three official routes: your local health department, which the EPA says may test for free; a state-certified drinking water laboratory, which the EPA and CDC say you should use; or a state or university program, such as Virginia's $70 VAHWQP clinic kit or Penn State's $60 standard package.

There are three main routes, and official sources point to each:

1. Your local health department

The EPA says your local health department may provide private well testing for free, and can tell you what substances are common in your area's groundwater. [source]

2. A state-certified laboratory

The EPA says to only use laboratories certified for drinking water testing, and to contact your state certified laboratory program or local health department to find one. [source]

3. A state or university program

Some states run subsidized programs, for example Virginia's drinking water clinics ($70 per kit) [source] and Penn State's lab (standard package $60). [source]

What to test for every year

EPA: total coliform bacteria, nitrates, total dissolved solids and pH, annually; test other contaminants if you suspect them. [source]

Test right away if…

  • there are known groundwater problems in your area
  • conditions near your well changed (flooding, land disturbance, new construction or industry)
  • you replaced or repaired any part of the well system
  • your water's odor, color or taste changed [source]

FAQ

What are my options for getting well water tested?

You have three official routes: your local health department, which the EPA says may test for free; a state-certified drinking water laboratory, which the EPA and CDC say you should use; or a state or university program, such as Virginia's $70 VAHWQP clinic kit or Penn State's $60 standard package.

How often should I test my well water?

The EPA recommends testing a private well every year for total coliform bacteria, nitrates, total dissolved solids and pH.

Can I get my well water tested for free?

The EPA says your local health department may provide private well testing for free. Availability varies, so contact your local health department.

What kind of lab should I use?

The EPA says to only use laboratories certified to do drinking water testing.
