Watch the timing on bacteria

Bacteria samples are time-sensitive. Penn State, for example, requires them to reach its lab within 30 hours of collection. [source] Check the lab's holding time before choosing a mail-in route.

FAQ

Should I use a mail-in kit, a certified lab or a county program?

Check your local health department first, since the EPA says it may test for free, then any state or university program near you. The EPA says to use a lab certified for drinking water testing. A mail-in kit suits people with no program nearby, but bacteria samples are time-sensitive, often within 30 hours.

Are mail-in well water kits reliable?

The EPA says to only use laboratories certified for drinking water testing. Check that any kit is analyzed by a certified or accredited lab and meets the bacteria holding time.

What is the cheapest way to test well water?

The EPA notes local health departments may test private wells for free. Some states also run low-cost programs, such as Virginia's $70 clinic kit.
