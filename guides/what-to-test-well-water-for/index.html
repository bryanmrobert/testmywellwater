What should you test well water for?

Short answer: Every year, test for total coliform bacteria, nitrates, total dissolved solids and pH, as the EPA and CDC recommend. Then add tests based on where you live: the CDC suggests asking your health department about volatile organic compounds, lead, arsenic, mercury, radium and pesticides, and the EPA ties nearby activities like farming or mining to specific tests.

Every year

  • Total coliform bacteria: an indicator that harmful germs may be present. You can also test for fecal coliform or E. coli; a positive result likely means feces have entered the well. [source]
  • Nitrates: the CDC says all well owners should test for nitrates at least once a year. [source]
  • Total dissolved solids and pH: indicators of scaling, taste and whether water may corrode pipes and release metals like lead. [source]

Ask your health department about

The CDC lists volatile organic compounds, lead, arsenic, mercury, radium, pesticides or herbicides, and other chemicals or germs, depending on where you live. [source]

FAQ

What should I test my well water for?

Every year, test for total coliform bacteria, nitrates, total dissolved solids and pH, as the EPA and CDC recommend. Then add tests based on where you live: the CDC suggests asking your health department about volatile organic compounds, lead, arsenic, mercury, radium and pesticides, and the EPA ties nearby activities like farming or mining to specific tests.

Does a standard well test include lead?

Not always. Penn State's $60 standard package covers coliform, E. coli, pH and TDS; lead is in its $125 package. Virginia's $70 VAHWQP kit includes lead.

Who can tell me what's common in my area?

The EPA and CDC both say your local health department can tell you which contaminants are common in your area's groundwater.
