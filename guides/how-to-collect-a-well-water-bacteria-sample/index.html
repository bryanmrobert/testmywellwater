How to collect a well water bacteria sample (step by step)

Short answer: Use the lab's sterile bottle, pick an inside faucet, remove the aerator and run cold water about five minutes. Without touching the inside of the bottle or cap, fill to the 100 mL line, cap it, refrigerate it and get it to the lab within 30 hours, per Penn State's instructions.

These steps follow Penn State AASL's instructions for its kit. Always follow your own lab's instructions, since bottles and holding times vary. [source]

  1. Use only the sterilized bacteria bottle (Penn State's has a green label). If it contains a small white pill or powder, leave it in.
  2. Choose an inside faucet and remove the aerator.
  3. Run the cold water for about five minutes to purge the plumbing.
  4. Remove the lid holding only the outside of the cap. Don't touch the inside of the bottle or cap.
  5. Fill to the 100 mL line and screw the lid on tightly.
  6. Refrigerate until shipping.
  7. Get it to the lab within 30 hours of collection. Penn State also requires arrival no later than Thursday of the week. [source]

Lead is different

Penn State's first-draw lead sample is the opposite: collect the first water out of the kitchen faucet in the morning after it has sat at least six hours, without running it first. [source]

The EPA also advises asking the lab for instructions and sample bottles before sampling, and testing again after replacing or repairing any part of the well system. [source]
