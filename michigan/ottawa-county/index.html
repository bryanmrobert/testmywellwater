Ottawa County, MI well water testing: county health department prices

Short answer: Pick up test bottles from the Ottawa County Department of Public Health at its Holland office (12251 James St., Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m.). Its fee schedule lists self-service tests at $20 for bacteria (coliform), $25 for partial chemical, $45 for both and $25 for lead. Call Environmental Health at 616-393-5645 to ask how to return the sample.

USGS's 2015 county estimate put Ottawa County's self-supplied population at about 75,000, roughly 27% of residents. USGS says the most common source of self-supplied water is a well. [source] No separate official county-wide private-well count was found. The county says its urbanized areas are served mainly by municipal systems that use Lake Michigan water, while its rural areas rely on water pumped from underground aquifers. [source]

The Ottawa County Department of Public Health offers water testing and analysis for private and public wells: bacteriological testing, partial chemical testing, and other tests whose cost varies with the analysis. [source]

What and how often to test

The county recommends testing your well once a year for total coliform bacteria, nitrates, total dissolved solids and pH. It also says to test if there are known well-water problems in your area, after flooding, land disturbance or nearby waste disposal, when you replace or repair any part of the well system, or when the water's taste, color or odor changes noticeably. [source]

Statewide, MDHHS recommends testing annually for coliform bacteria and E. coli, nitrate and nitrite; every 3 to 5 years for arsenic and lead; and at least once for PFAS. [source] See our nitrate guide and coliform guide.

Local issue: salty water from the bedrock aquifer

Ottawa County commissioned Michigan State University to study its groundwater. The study found that water levels in the deep bedrock aquifer have been declining for 20 years and that sodium chloride (salt) levels are rising above recommended standards in some areas. [source] Before 2000, generally fewer than 4% of groundwater samples in the county had chloride above 250 mg/L, the recommended standard; from 2000 to 2010, 6–10% did. Chloride rose with depth in both the glacial and bedrock aquifers, which the study says points to a deep underground source rather than surface contamination such as road salt. [source]

If your well draws from the deep bedrock (Marshall) aquifer or your water tastes salty, ask the county or a certified lab about adding chloride or sodium to your test.

Buying or selling a home with a well

Since 1984, the Ottawa County Environmental Health Code has required sellers to have private well and septic systems evaluated before a property changes hands. The county gives the evaluation report to buyers at closing. [source]

New wells

A private single-family new or replacement well permit is $555.00 and includes up to four site visits. A first resample of a new well after a positive coliform result is $20.00. [source]

State lab and other certified labs

Michigan EGLE's state lab fee schedule, effective October 1, 2026, lists total coliform and E. coli at $23, nitrate and nitrite at $41, arsenic or lead at $15 each plus a $13 sample-preparation fee, volatile organic compounds at $147, and PFAS at $320. Call 517-335-8184 to order a kit. [source] [source] [source] You can also search EGLE's laboratory certification lists. [source]

FAQ

Where can I get my well water tested in Ottawa County, Michigan?

Pick up test bottles from the Ottawa County Department of Public Health at its Holland office (12251 James St., Monday–Friday 8 a.m.–5 p.m.). Its fee schedule lists self-service tests at $20 for bacteria (coliform), $25 for partial chemical, $45 for both and $25 for lead. How much does a well water test cost in Ottawa County, MI?

The Ottawa County Department of Public Health's fee schedule, effective October 1, 2026, lists self-service tests at $20 for bacteria (coliform), $25 for partial chemical, $45 for bacteria and partial chemical, and $25 for lead. The county says these are subject to change based on the current lab rate.

Where do I get a well water test bottle in Ottawa County?

Test bottles are available at the county health department's Holland office, 12251 James St., Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. (closed 12:15 to 1:15 p.m.). Call 616-393-5645 for more information.

Do I need a well inspection to sell a house in Ottawa County, MI?

Yes. Since 1984 the county Environmental Health Code has required sellers to have private well and septic systems evaluated before transfer. A private water supply evaluation is $371, and selling without a completed evaluation adds $250 to the fee.

Is salt a problem in Ottawa County well water?

In some areas. A Michigan State University study for the county found chloride above 250 mg/L in 6–10% of groundwater samples from 2000 to 2010, up from under 4% before 2000, mostly tied to the deep bedrock aquifer.
