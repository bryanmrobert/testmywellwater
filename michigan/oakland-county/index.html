Oakland County, MI well water testing: county lab and state lab prices

Short answer: Pick up sample bottles at an Oakland County Health Division office in Pontiac (248-858-1312) or Southfield (248-424-7000); its certified lab tests for bacteria, nitrate, nitrite, arsenic, lead and copper. Or order a kit from Michigan EGLE's lab at 517-335-8184, where a coliform and E. coli test costs $23. Test yearly for bacteria and nitrate.

A 2004 U.S. Geological Survey fact sheet, produced in cooperation with Oakland County, estimated that about 233,000 county residents use private wells for household drinking water, and that over 95 percent of wells in the county draw from the shallow glacial aquifer. [source] USGS's 2015 county estimate put the self-supplied population at about 218,000. [source]

The Oakland County Health Division says private wells aren't tested regularly for contaminants and that testing is the well owner's responsibility. [source]

How to use the EGLE lab

  • Order: call 517-335-8184 for a kit. EGLE ships it or you can pick it up by appointment; allow 5–7 business days to process the order, plus shipping. [source]
  • Return: get samples and forms to the lab within 24 hours of collection. Drop off in person at 3350 N. Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd., Lansing (schedule first at 517-335-8184), or ship overnight Monday–Wednesday. Coliform and E. coli samples aren't accepted on Fridays. [source]
  • Results: allow up to 10 business days. EGLE also sends results to the local health department for the county where the sample was collected, and says to call your local health department to interpret them. [source]

What and how often to test

The Oakland County Health Division recommends sampling a private well every year for bacteria, nitrate and nitrite, and every few years for arsenic. [source] Statewide, MDHHS also recommends testing for PFAS at least once; see our Michigan guide. [source]

If coliform is found

Until the problem is fixed or a sample shows no coliform, the county says to use another water source, such as bottled water, for drinking, cooking, baby formula, brushing teeth and anything else you might swallow. If that isn't possible, bring the water to a full rolling boil for one minute. Resample before making any physical changes to the water system; if a second sample also finds coliform, contact a licensed well contractor to evaluate, repair and sanitize the well and plumbing. [source] See our coliform guide.

Nitrate and arsenic

Nitrate: the county says results above 10 mg/L nitrate or 1 mg/L nitrite can be dangerous to infants and pregnant women. Don't boil the water, which concentrates nitrate, and standard pitcher carbon filters don't remove it; reverse osmosis, ion exchange and distillation do. Until it's fixed, use another source for formula, cooking and drinking. [source] See our nitrate guide.

Arsenic: the county notes that Michigan has naturally higher arsenic levels in groundwater and that arsenic has no taste, smell or color. Above 10 parts per billion (0.010 mg/L), use another source for drinking, cooking and brushing teeth until treatment certified for arsenic is in place. Options include under-sink reverse osmosis, adsorptive media or distillation, whole-house treatment, or a new well after consulting the Health Division. [source]

New or replacement wells

Oakland County lists a $260 permit for a new private well on vacant land and $238 for a replacement well. Some well permit applications can be submitted online. [source]

Other certified labs

You can also use a private lab certified for the test you need; see EGLE's laboratory certification lists. [source] The county's arsenic fact sheet advises confirming the lab is certified for arsenic in drinking water before you test. [source]
